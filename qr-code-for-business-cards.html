How to add a QR code to your business card

  1. Open the Contact tab — it builds a vCard, the format phones understand as "a person".
  2. Fill in your details: name, phone, email, company and website.
  3. Generate and scan it yourself first. Check the contact card that appears on the phone says what you expect.
  4. Place it on the card at 2cm or larger, with a few millimetres of clear space around it.

vCard or a link to your website?

A vCard saves your details even with no signal, works forever, and needs no hosting. The catch is that it is fixed: change jobs or numbers and the printed cards are wrong.

A link to your site — or a LinkedIn profile — can be updated without reprinting, but needs a connection, adds a step before anything is saved, and depends on that page continuing to exist.

For most people a vCard is the better trade, because a box of business cards is usually replaced long before it runs out anyway. If you change roles often, point the code at a page you control instead.

Designing it onto the card

2cm minimum

Below that, a vCard's denser pattern starts failing on older cameras. If it will not fit, cut a field — dropping the website often buys enough room.

Leave the quiet zone

Designers love to butt the code against an edge or run text right up to it. Scanners need a clear margin to find where the pattern ends.

Watch the card stock

Heavy texture and high-gloss finishes both scatter light. A matt or lightly coated stock reads far more reliably.

Keep the contrast

A tasteful pale grey code on a white card may look elegant and scan terribly. Keep the pattern genuinely dark.

What to leave out

Everything in the code is readable by anyone who scans it, and business cards get passed around. A work number and work email are fine. A home address or personal mobile you would rather not hand to strangers are not — and once the cards are printed, you cannot take it back.
